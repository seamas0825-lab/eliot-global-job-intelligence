# Company Intelligence

Build a company model that is sufficient to explain the role, not a generic company profile.

## Research branches

| Branch | Inspect | Decision affected |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Product | product behavior, pricing, catalog, reviews, onboarding, proof | value proposition, buyer, economics |
| Business | business model, geography, distribution, partners, stage signals | why hire now, outcome owned |
| Employer | JD, careers, leadership, public employee information | team interfaces, role maturity |
| Market | local demand, platform behavior, regulation, direct competitors, business-model peers, mechanism analogues | operating chain, benchmark logic, and localization |
| Reputation | user/employee reviews, complaints, support patterns | risk, trust, interview questions |

Prefer official and original evidence; use third-party summaries as discovery leads. Observe dates because product, pricing, staff, and strategy change.

Company research does not pass merely because the hiring company's website and social accounts were opened. For brand-side roles, complete the competitor operating-system work in `benchmark-intelligence.md` before finalizing the role model.

## Required output

For each material conclusion record:

```text
ID:
Claim:
Status: VERIFIED FACT | INFERENCE | UNKNOWN
Direct URL or candidate asset:
Observed date:
Supporting evidence:
Contradiction or alternative:
Confidence:
Decision affected:
Reversal condition:
```

## Missing public presence

Absence of a public account, price, case study, or employee page is not proof that none exists. Record the retrieval scope and date. Use the absence only as an operating constraint: for example, public proof could not be verified, so the candidate should ask how acquisition currently works.

## Interview-useful synthesis

End with:

- one sentence on what the company sells, to whom, and why they buy;
- the strongest verified reason the role exists now;
- two plausible business problems, clearly labeled as inference;
- three contradictions or unknowns worth asking about;
- the result the candidate should position around.
- the comparable-brand set and the most useful operating-chain lesson.
