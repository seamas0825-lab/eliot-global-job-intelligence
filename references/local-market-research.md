# Local Market Research

Research the market where the work happens, not the language in which the report is written.

## Local Market Gate

Record:

```text
Country/decision-useful region:
Primary and secondary languages:
Audience/customer and buying context:
Platform/channel ecosystem:
Commercial model and common partnership norms:
Payment, contract, and communication habits:
Applicable platform, advertising, privacy, consumer, or employment constraints:
Chinese experience that transfers:
Chinese experience that requires adaptation:
Evidence gaps and local validation needed:
```

Do not stop at “overseas,” “global,” “欧美,” “Europe,” or “Southeast Asia” when country-level differences can change the workflow.

## Build local queries

Use product/category terms plus native user language:

- problem, outcome, comparison, review, complaint, risk, price, and alternative terms;
- local equivalents of “how to,” “worth it,” “before you,” “things I wish I knew,” and “mistakes”;
- buyer-role titles, platform-native terminology, and local spelling;
- city, country, language, currency, contract, shipping, tax, or safety modifiers when material.

Preserve original wording and add a close translation. Do not translate Chinese assumptions into English and treat them as local demand evidence.

## Test transferability

For each Chinese-market practice ask:

1. Is the audience tension the same?
2. Is the proof acceptable and credible locally?
3. Are channel mechanics and distribution comparable?
4. Are consent, disclosure, data, contract, and payment norms compatible?
5. Does the candidate know the difference well enough to explain it?

Classify as `directly_transferable`, `transferable_with_adaptation`, `mechanism_only`, or `not_supported`.

## Thin evidence

If local-language access is weak, do not substitute English-only search and claim market validation. Record the language/access limit, use a mechanism analogy only as a hypothesis, and prepare a validation plan with a local colleague, customer, creator, or interview question.
