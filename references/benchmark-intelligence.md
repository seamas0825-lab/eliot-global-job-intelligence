# Benchmark and Competitive Operating-system Intelligence

Do not jump from the hiring company to a list of individual creators, posts, prospects, ads, or listings. First reconstruct how comparable organizations in the named market perform the same commercial job.

## Benchmark ladder

Use three layers:

1. **Direct competitors:** similar product, customer, price logic, and target market.
2. **Business-model peers:** similar buying cycle, creator economics, conversion path, or distribution model even if the category differs.
3. **Mechanism analogues:** best-in-class brands that solve the same trust, proof, education, affiliate, paid-amplification, or retention problem.

Label the layer. Do not call every useful analogy a competitor.

## Standard planning guardrail

When public evidence permits, inspect:

- 3–5 direct competitor or category brands;
- 2–3 business-model/mechanism analogues;
- 3–6 recent material campaigns, creator programs, or repeated content patterns;
- the individual role-specific samples required by the role router;
- 3–6 current tool/platform categories only when tool choice affects the work.

These are coverage guardrails, not proof thresholds. Fewer strong cases are better than invented or irrelevant quota fillers.

## Brand operating-system record

For each `BRAND-*` capture:

```text
Brand, market, product and price logic
Target audience and buying tension
Creator/partner objective
Creator mix and visible selection pattern
Sourcing or program-entry path
Collaboration model: gifting | flat fee | affiliate | ambassador | hybrid
Repeated content and proof mechanisms
Briefing, disclosure, usage-rights, or whitelisting signals
Organic, affiliate, shop, DTC, and paid-amplification connection
Public tracking or attribution signals
Renewal, ambassador, or recurring-program signals
Direct source URLs and observation dates
What is verified, inferred, unknown, and non-transferable
```

Do not infer internal rates, workflow, budgets, ROI, or attribution from visible posts alone.

## Campaign-chain reconstruction

Reconstruct at least one chain:

```text
Business objective → creator persona → discovery/program entry → qualification
→ deal model → brief/proof mechanism → publish → paid/affiliate/shop/DTC handoff
→ measurement signal → renewal or stop
```

For every step state the public evidence, inference, missing internal data, and lesson for the hiring company. A campaign montage is not an operating-chain analysis.

## Tool and platform landscape

Research tools only when the role depends on tool choice or the user asks. For Creator/KOL work, useful categories include creator discovery, social listening, outreach/CRM, affiliate tracking, gifting/logistics, contracts/rights, payment, paid amplification, and measurement.

For each current tool/platform record:

- job it performs;
- target customer and market coverage;
- official feature evidence and verified date;
- advantage in this workflow;
- limitation, data-access risk, or integration dependency;
- fit for the hiring company and role;
- cheaper/manual alternative.

Use Grok to discover current practitioner discussion, recently adopted tools, expert workflows, and AI-driven marketing products. Verify material capabilities on official product pages. Do not produce an undifferentiated “top tools” list.

## Benchmark Intelligence Gate

- **PASS:** comparable brands and campaigns are present, at least one commercial operating chain is reconstructed, lessons are source-linked, and individual samples are interpreted in that context.
- **PROVISIONAL:** public evidence supports brand/campaign patterns but not a complete chain; state the missing links and interview questions.
- **BLOCKED:** output contains only company facts and individual samples, or merely lists competitor names without explaining how the work is done.

The final brief must answer: who is comparable, how they appear to run the mechanism, what repeatedly works, what cannot be inferred, and what the candidate should borrow or challenge.
